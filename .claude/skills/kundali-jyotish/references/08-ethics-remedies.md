# Ethics, sensitive questions and remedies

## Why this matters
People ask astrologers about illness, death, infertility, failing marriages, debts and exams,
often when anxious. A confident-sounding line ("ye shaadi nahi chalegi", "is saal bada accident")
can change real decisions or cause real fear. The tradition itself warns astrologers against
predicting death and against frightening the querent. Being careful is part of being expert.

## Handling sensitive requests

| Request | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| "Kitne saal jeeyenge?" / death timing (self or relative) | Say kindly that jyotish maryada forbids predicting death and no one can know it; acknowledge the worry; offer: periods that call for extra care and periods that are supportive; practical support ideas | give dates, ages, "maraka = death", Ayu calculations |
| Serious illness | Encourage following the doctors fully; describe supportive vs demanding periods in general terms; suggest optional comfort practices (prayer, mantra, seva) alongside treatment | diagnose, predict recovery/non-recovery, suggest stopping/delaying treatment, promise a remedy will cure |
| Pregnancy, children, infertility | gentle language; timing windows framed as "favourable"; medical guidance | predict infertility, sex of the child, miscarriage |
| Marriage problems / divorce | describe patterns and supportive periods; suggest counselling and communication | declare the marriage doomed or advise divorce |
| Compatibility / Manglik | full picture with cancellations | "do not marry" on astrology alone |
| Money, trading, lottery | general periods of gains/expenses; prudence | stock tips, numbers, "guaranteed" profit |
| Exams, jobs | promise + windows + effort advice | "you will definitely fail/pass" |
| Legal cases | supportive/difficult periods; lawyer | outcome guarantees |
| Third person (partner, boss) | general, respectful | private predictions about someone who has not consented |
| Mental health crisis / self-harm hints | respond with care first; encourage reaching a trusted person or a local helpline/professional immediately; astrology comes second, if at all | treat it as just an astrology question |

Example wording for the death question (Hinglish):
> Main samajh sakta hoon ki aap kitne chintit hain. Jyotish ki parampara mein aayu ya mrityu ka
> samay batana manaa hai, aur sach yeh hai ki koi bhi ise nishchit roop se nahi jaan sakta. Main
> aapko yeh bata sakta hoon ki kundli ke hisaab se kaun sa samay zyada dekhbhaal maangta hai aur
> kaun sa sahayak hai - aur sabse zaroori, doctors ki salah ke saath poora sahyog dein.

## Remedies (upay) - order of preference

1. **Behavioural** (free, no side effects): the planet's virtues - Saturn: discipline, serving the
   needy, punctuality; Sun: respect father/authority, morning sunlight, honesty; Moon: sleep,
   hydration, time with mother, calm routine; Mars: exercise, anger management; Mercury: learning,
   clear communication; Jupiter: teaching, respecting gurus, charity in education; Venus: art,
   cleanliness, respect for spouse/women; Rahu: digital discipline, avoiding shortcuts; Ketu:
   meditation, completing unfinished tasks.
2. **Devotional** (if the person is comfortable): the planet's mantra (e.g. "Om Shanaishcharaya
   Namah" for Saturn), Hanuman Chalisa for Mars/Saturn periods, Aditya Hridayam for the Sun,
   Vishnu Sahasranama for Jupiter/Mercury. Respect the person's own faith; offer
   faith-neutral alternatives (meditation, gratitude, service).
3. **Daan and seva** (modest): planet-linked charity on its weekday (Saturn - Saturday, food/
   blankets for workers; Moon - Monday, milk/water; Sun - Sunday, wheat/jaggery). Keep it small;
   no prescription of expensive items.
4. **Gemstones** - only if the person asks or the chart strongly supports it:
   - Recommend only stones of functional benefics that are weak (usually Lagna lord, 5th or 9th
     lord, yogakaraka). Never for 6/8/12 lords or functional malefics; never Blue Sapphire
     casually.
   - Say clearly: gemstones are a traditional belief, not a proven treatment; they never replace
     medical care, and "a stone to cure an illness" is not something you can promise.
   - Suggest trying behavioural/devotional remedies first; if they still want a stone, a modest,
     genuine stone after consulting a trusted astrologer.
   - If the report recommends a stone, check it against the functional analysis and say whether
     it fits.
5. **Pujas/rituals**: mention only if the person asks; no pressure, no "must do or else".

> Jyotish ek paramparik margdarshan hai; yeh sambhavnaayein aur samay ki disha batata hai, nishchit
> bhavishya nahi. Swasthya, paisa ya kanooni faislon ke liye sambandhit professional ki salah
> zaroor lein.
